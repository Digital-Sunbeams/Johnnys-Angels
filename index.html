Why people call

Nine hours is a long time for a five-pound dog.

Most of our families work full days. They're not looking for a kennel with forty dogs in it — they want somewhere their dog is actually happy to be dropped off, and somewhere they can leave for a week without feeling guilty about it.

That's the whole idea here. Small groups, small dogs, indoors and out, and one person who knows every dog by name and by temperament.

No two dogs, no two days

Every dog gets their own plan.

Before the first day, Natasha asks a lot of questions — what your dog eats and when, who they like, what scares them, how they sleep, what you'd normally do at four in the afternoon. Then she runs the day that way.

Their routine, kept

Meals at your times, medication on your schedule, the same walk length they're used to. A dog whose day still feels familiar comes home settled, happy, and ready for the evening with you.

Their pace

Some dogs want the whole afternoon on a lap. Some want three walks. Seniors get more rest, puppies get more structure, and nobody gets forced into a playgroup.

Their quirks, accommodated

Won't eat unless someone sits nearby. Hates the vacuum. Only drinks from a specific bowl. Explain it once, and it will be written down and followed.

About

Johnny's Angels started with Johnny.

Natasha has been looking after small dogs in Summerlin for years — first for neighbors, then for their friends, and eventually for enough families that it needed a name. It took the name of her own dog.

She isn't running a facility. She's running a home that happens to have four or five small dogs in it, which is a different thing entirely. There's no intake counter, no webcam feed of a concrete run, and no teenager on the closing shift — just one person who has been doing this long enough to know that a dog hiding behind the sofa on day one is normal, and that the same dog will be first out the door by day four.

Her place is a quiet, air-conditioned apartment with a shaded patio, in a neighborhood that's genuinely pleasant to walk. For small dogs that turns out to be better than a yard: they get two real walks a day with someone paying attention, instead of being let out a door alone. Indoors they're on the couch, not on concrete.

She takes dogs other people find complicated: the ten-year-old with three medications, the rescue who doesn't like men, the terrier who has to be the only one on the chair. Every dog here runs on their own schedule rather than a house schedule. Small dogs have opinions — that's most of the fun.

And yes — there's usually classic rock on low in the kitchen. Nobody has complained yet, least of all the dogs.

House rules

What we don't do

A short list, and the reason for each one.

No big dogs

Even the friendliest sixty-pound dog changes how a five-pound dog moves through a room. The size cap is what makes the rest work.

No overbooking

Six is the ceiling. If the day is full, you'll be told it's full — you won't be squeezed in and neither will anyone else's dog.

No all-day crating

Crates are for naps and only for dogs who like them. Nobody spends the day behind a door.

Questions

Asked and answered

How big can my dog be?

Twenty-five pounds and under. It's a firm line — the whole environment is built around dogs that size, and it's what lets timid little dogs relax here.

What do I need to bring the first day?

Food portioned for the day, any medication with instructions, and their leash. Vaccine records can be emailed ahead of time. Beds, bowls, towels, and toys are already here.

What vaccines do you require?

Current rabies, DHPP, and Bordetella. Send the records once and they stay on file.

My dog is anxious and doesn't like other dogs. Is this a bad idea?

Often it's the opposite. Small groups, a quiet home, and no large dogs are exactly what anxious dogs need. Come for a meet-and-greet — if it isn't right, you'll hear that honestly rather than be sold a day.

Can you give medication?

Yes, at no extra charge — pills, liquids, insulin, eye drops, and the ones that need to be hidden in cheese. Just write down the timing.

Do you take puppies or senior dogs?

Both. Puppies start with a short trial day, and seniors get a quieter corner and as many naps as they want.

What about holidays?

Boarding runs seven days a week, holidays included, but the calendar fills early. Thanksgiving and the winter holidays are usually spoken for months ahead.

How do I pay?

Cash, Venmo, or Zelle at pickup for daycare. Stays longer than a week take a deposit up front and the balance at pickup.

What if my dog isn't a good fit?

You'll be told, kindly and quickly. A dog who's miserable here isn't good for anyone, and there are other places in town better suited to some dogs.

What do dogs like best about being here?

Being included. They're in the room where things are actually happening — the kitchen, the couch, the walk — instead of waiting somewhere for the day to end. Most dogs work that out by the second visit, and start pulling toward the door at drop-off.
